## How the two onboardings fit together

The company onboarding runs three weeks and everyone does it. Your role onboarding runs alongside it and then keeps going.

For the first three weeks, mornings belong to the company onboarding and its gates. Afternoons belong to this. That is deliberate. Three weeks of classroom before you touch a phone produces someone who is frightened of the phone.

| Week | Mornings | Afternoons |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Company onboarding, gate one | Read pages one to three here. Listen to five recorded calls. Shadow three live ones |
| 2 | Company onboarding, gate two | Build one agent yourself. First supervised dials, two a day |
| 3 | Company onboarding, gate three | Write your first sequence. Review it before it sends |
| 4 | Role certification | Own the full daily cadence |

By the end of week four you are running live and unsupervised. Everything after that is engines, volume and coaching.

## Before your first day

This is on your manager rather than on you, and it should all be done before you arrive. Chasing logins for three days is a management failure, not an onboarding activity.

- [ ] **Access provisioned and tested.** HubSpot, Amplemarket, Salesfinity, Attio, PostHog, Slack, Notion, Clay, August itself
- [ ] **Email warmed** and connected, sending domain checked
- [ ] **Phone number and voicemail live**
- [ ] **Laptop, monitor and a proper headset** received
- [ ] **Territory assigned in writing.** Which segments, which regions, which accounts are off limits because an AE already owns them
- [ ] **Quota and ramp numbers written down** and shared before day one
- [ ] **An onboarding owner and a peer buddy who is not your manager**
- [ ] **Week one calendar fully booked**, with a named owner on every session

---

## Certification, end of week four

You are cleared to run outbound unsupervised when you can do all five of these. Two attempts allowed on the mock call.

- [ ] **The fifteen second opener,** cold, flexed three ways for a litigator, a corporate associate and a COO
- [ ] **A live mock call** from cold open through discovery through one objection to a booked meeting
- [ ] **A cold email your manager would send without editing it**
- [ ] **The nearest-reference test.** Given six firm profiles, name the right reference for each and say why
- [ ] **The roadmap traps.** Three planted *can August do X* questions where the correct answer is a roadmap deflection rather than a yes

## Where your leads come from, and for how long

This changes deliberately over your first six months. The point is that you learn what a good conversation sounds like before you spend your time deciding who to have one with.

| Period | Fed to you | Built by you | What you are learning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–8 | 100% | Nothing | What a qualified conversation actually sounds like |
| Weeks 9–12 | About half | About half | Whether you can find firms as good as the ones we gave you |
| Month 4+ | About a third | About two thirds | Running your own territory |

The leads you get in the first eight weeks are real and they are good. They are not practice. If you burn them with a generic sequence you have burned an account we cannot get back, so the review before your sequences go live exists for a reason.

## Quota

A **qualified meeting** means somebody sat for a demo or moved past discovery. A booking that no-showed does not count. A *send me something* does not count. This is the same definition your comp uses, on purpose, so that coaching and pay are measuring the same thing.

| Period | Qualified meetings per month | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–4 | Certification instead of volume | Gates and the mock call |
| Weeks 5–8 | 4 | One engine at a time, warmest first |
| Weeks 9–12 | 7 | All three engines running |
| Month 4+ | 10 | Full number |

## The scorecard

Volume is not the interesting part of this job, so it is not most of the scorecard. Reviewed weekly from week five.

| What we look at | Red | Amber | Green |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified meetings per month, at full ramp | Under 6 | 6 to 9 | 10 or more |
| Booked meetings that actually happen | Under 60% | 60 to 75% | Over 75% |
| Handoffs the AE accepts without follow-up questions | Under 60% | 60 to 80% | Over 80% |
| Reply rate on personalised sequences | Under 4% | 4 to 8% | Over 8% |
| Dials that turn into a real conversation | Under 5% | 5 to 8% | Over 8% |
| Accounts worked with genuine personalisation each week | Under 25 | 25 to 40 | Over 40 |
| Something written and posted for the team | None | Occasional | Every week |

**Two of these matter more than the rest.** Held rate tells you whether the person you booked understood what they were coming to. Handoff acceptance tells you whether the meeting was worth having. Those two together are the actual measure of this job, and they are the two an SDR chasing volume will quietly destroy.

## The quality gates

A meeting can hit the number and still be a bad meeting. These are the things that get a meeting sent back.

- The partner arrived expecting a chatbot demo
- The firm is outside the segment, and nobody flagged it
- The person on the call cannot buy and cannot introduce the person who can
- The handoff was a name and a time
- Something was promised that we do not do today

One of these in a month is a coaching conversation. Three is a pattern, and we deal with a pattern by looking at your calls together rather than by adjusting the number.

## The first ninety days

| | What we are looking for |
|---|---|
| **Day 30** | Certified. First meetings booked from the site visitor engine. You can tell three customer stories from memory, in our words rather than the website's |
| **Day 60** | Meetings from at least two engines. Your first self-built list has been reviewed and it is good. One thing you have built and published for the team |
| **Day 90** | Full quota in sight. A written thirty-minute readout on which engine converts best in your territory, with the numbers behind the claim |

The day ninety review runs against [Good SDR, Bad SDR](./good-sdr-bad-sdr.md). For each area, ownership, market, product, where your job stops, targeting, messaging, the phone, systems, discipline, we say where you sit on the good and bad axis and show the evidence for it.

Nothing in that review will be the first time you have heard it.

## What your manager owes you

This part is not optional either. Horowitz's standard is that you cannot fairly hold somebody to expectations you never taught them, and the burden of proof is on the manager.

- Every session on the schedule happens, with the named owner in the room. If the owner cannot make it, the session moves rather than quietly disappearing
- A weekly one-to-one that is never cancelled and is structured against [Good SDR, Bad SDR](./good-sdr-bad-sdr.md) rather than being a status update
- Written feedback on a recorded call every week through week eight
- Territory and quota in writing before your first day
- The hard thing said on Wednesday, not saved up for the ninety day review

If any of these is not happening, raise it. That is not a complaint, it is the process working.
