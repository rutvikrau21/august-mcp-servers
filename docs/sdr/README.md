## What this job actually is

Your job is to get qualified meetings with law firms that should be talking to us and are not yet.

That sounds simple and it is not, because of how we sell. Most software companies hire SDRs to book demos, and the demo does the work. We do not sell like that. Our sale is consultative, it is closer to advising a firm on its business than to showing them a product, and the person who eventually closes it will spend hours talking to a managing partner about where his margin comes from.

Which means the meeting you book is only worth something if the partner arrives interested in the right thing. Booking a meeting where a partner turns up expecting a chatbot demo is worse than booking nothing, because we then spend the first ten minutes correcting a frame you set.

So your job is not really to book meetings. It is to **create the right expectation in the right person and get it into the calendar.**

## Where your job stops

This matters as much as what your job is.

You work **step one** prospects almost exclusively. Firms that do not have us yet, that want a better way of working, that need to see the platform. That is the overwhelming majority of the market and it is where every one of our customers started.

You do not run the partnership conversation. You do not talk about joint ventures, revenue share or holding companies. Not because it is secret, but because that conversation only works when there is evidence behind it, and on a cold call there is none. A partner who hears it too early concludes we are trying to buy his firm, and that is very hard to walk back.

> **The line to hold.** You can gesture at where this goes. You should not try to get there on the call.
>
> Saying *"the firms we work with usually start with the platform, and some of them end up building client-facing products on top of it"* is good. It signals we are not a tool vendor and it makes a partner curious.
>
> Saying *"we could set up a joint venture and you could raise capital against your agents"* on a first call is bad, however true it is. Hand that to an AE with the context that makes it credible.

## The one thing that makes this job different here

Most SDR roles are a volume game with a fixed script. This one is not, for a specific reason.

We sell to a market where nobody buys from a company they have not heard of, and where the single most persuasive thing in our arsenal is a story about a firm down the road. That means the quality of your targeting and the specificity of your message matter far more than your dial count. Our best deal came from a campaign that reached about sixty people.

You will still make a lot of calls. But sixty firms you can genuinely say something specific to will beat six hundred you cannot, every time.
