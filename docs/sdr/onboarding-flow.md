## The principle

You will be on the phone in week two. Not week five.

Three weeks of classroom before you touch a phone produces somebody who's frightened of the phone, and you already know how to have a conversation with a stranger. What you don't yet have is our language, our proof and our angle. That's what the first two weeks are for, and the fastest way to absorb them is to hear them said out loud by the people who say them for a living.

# The four weeks

### Week 1 · Language and proof

Mornings on the company onboarding. Afternoons here.

- [ ] Tool access checklist from BDR Onboarding, done by day two
- [ ] **Work through all sixteen clips in the call library below.** Write one page on what surprised you
- [ ] Read [Good SDR, Bad SDR](./good-sdr-bad-sdr.md) and write which pairs describe you today and which don't
- [ ] Listen to three full calls end to end, including one that went badly
- [ ] Shadow two live calls

### Week 2 · Product, and the phone

- [ ] Build an agent yourself, run a playbook against a real contract, batch a document set into a tabular review
- [ ] Read [The Outbound Craft](./outbound-craft.md) and [Where Your Leads Come From](./lead-sources.md)
- [ ] Go back to sections 1 and 2 of the call library and write out the pitch in your own words
- [ ] **First supervised dials. Two a day, with your manager listening.** The goal isn't meetings, it's saying the words out loud
- [ ] Sit in on one Monday build with Shaya

### Week 3 · Own a list

- [ ] Take one theme from [Outbound Focus](https://app.notion.com/p/3899073ab18780259b1fcaab5589f8d7) and build the list yourself
- [ ] Write all five assets. Reviewed before anything sends
- [ ] Run your own call blocks unsupervised
- [ ] Post the daily update every day

### Week 4 · Certify and go live

Two attempts allowed on the mock call.

- [ ] **The fifteen-second opener**, cold, flexed for a litigator, a corporate associate and a COO
- [ ] **A live mock call**, cold open through discovery through one objection to a booked meeting
- [ ] **A cold email your manager would send without editing**
- [ ] **The nearest-reference test.** Six firm profiles, name the right story for each and say why
- [ ] **The roadmap traps.** Three planted *can August do X* questions where the right answer is a deflection rather than a yes
- [ ] **The angle test.** Explain in one sentence why a firm picks us over Harvey, without naming a feature

## One thing to keep listening for

Every week, pick one recorded call and listen to it properly before anyone asks you to. Note the language the customer used rather than the language we used.

That habit is the difference between somebody who learns the script and somebody who learns the market.
